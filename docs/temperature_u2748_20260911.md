## Fixed Sampling Seed

Fixed environment seeds 1511000000 and shop_router_0909, using 16 newly sampled seeds, covering dual positions, totaling 32 games, all won; terminal cash amounts are **115,348–137,915**, with a standard deviation of **5,809**, and **19 distinct action programs** observed in the first three days.
This is a sensitivity check for a single fixed scenario and cannot be regarded as the overall win rate of a new seed strategy.

Fixed seeds are suitable for reproducibility and pairing evaluation. Online, if executing a random strategy with T=1, a new sampling seed should be generated for each game, used continuously within the game, and recorded to avoid the same seat repeatedly following the same random sequence. However, with a fixed initial observation and an additional 128 seeds drawn, **126 times the same first-turn action is selected**: the opening fixation arises from inherent concentration in the model's distribution, and changing seeds does not automatically resolve opening issues. In this case, no favorable seeds were chosen, and no modifications to the deployment were implemented.

## Two Abnormal Opening Turns

The user-specified **107807755 / 107806761** both play in the second seat against ymg_aq, with the first **72 moves** identical.
The additionally downloaded game against the same opponent, **107810794**, matches the first **145 moves** of the first game.
Compared to games against other opponents, our team's first four moves are identical, diverging from the fifth move onward.

Transaction item-by-item verification confirms complete consistency with the official initial two-step state: in the abnormal game, before buying sheep, there were only **988**; buying two sheep requires **1,000**, leaving a shortfall of **12**, so only one transaction occurs, with **488** remaining. In contrast, in the referenced game, two sheep are bought and **54** remain. Maintaining the initial state and our first two steps unchanged, removing only the opponent's first-round wheat buy/sell transactions restores the outcome of buying two sheep. This is a confirmed trigger point for a branching at the opening phase, not a counterfactual attribution of the final game outcome.

After the fifth step of the less-than-lamb scenario, the action distribution of the fourth helper becomes noticeably dispersed; under the same observational sampling of 128 times, there are 41 instances of DIG. Under normal control conditions, this helper receives exactly one lamb in all 128 observations. The first two instructions at T=0 on these initial observations are identical to those at T=1, thus the temperature itself does not eliminate the funding threshold.

This supports "reproducible, broad-range, and early opponent profit opportunities in opening weaknesses," but remains only a two-turn experiment, and cannot derive a targeted win rate for a full game, nor prove that opponents are deliberately targeting our side. Results are available in [Opening Perturbation Report](../outputs/analysis/opening-stress-u2748-20260911/report.json), with entry point `kagg_revue.evaluation.temperature.stress`, and new modules Ruff, mypy have passed.

Suggest that in future, the trading volume, sequence, and timing in the early turns be altered, followed by a full execution of the native script on the competitive panel for validation;
Integrate the states resulting from effective perturbations and insufficient actual trades into the training process, while retaining coverage from normal opponents.
The goal is to enable reasonable resource reallocation and maintain win rate even after procurement failure. The current market decoder lacks explicit order prefix cash/inventory ledgers; further improvements could include adding budget features and consistent feasibility constraints on gains.
These suggestions have not yet been implemented in training or deployment.

## Verification and Result Trajectory

- Full Ruff, mypy (700 source files) passes.
- From five replays, the first 96 steps of each are taken, totaling 480 states; T1's actor and eager models, as well as T0's exported model and eager argmax, are consistent.
- Native batch observation and in-package observation extensions are implemented; with two seats, 192 individual-step comparisons pass.
- Local environment still fails to fully reproduce online random actions: in three games against the same opponent, the first 96 steps differ by 40, 41, and 40 steps respectively, and in two additional games, there are 2 and 3 steps of difference. Rechecking with Python 3.12 / Torch 2.12 and AVX2 still reveals the same early inconsistencies; the source remains undetermined, and local win rate cannot be considered a faithful per-game reproduction of online behavior.
- 107807755's 719-step runtime log has no stderr; cold start takes 12.276 seconds, with subsequent maximum latency of 0.573 seconds.
