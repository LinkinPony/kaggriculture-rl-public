## Is KL Anchoring Necessary?

User asks whether KL anchoring slows down training. Conclusion is to keep 0.3, user agrees.

- **The wall-clock cost is approximately 13%.** Anchoring requires an additional forward pass per step against a reference policy. For s100901 and s101101 with KL = 0.3, each update takes 43 seconds (learner: 29.5 seconds); similarly, the two-GPU, 128-game, non-anchored runs s100201 and s100301 take 37–38 seconds (learner: 24–25 seconds).
- **Slow early progress is primarily due to warmup, not anchoring.** At U10, the actor learning rate is only 3% of its peak, with a reference KL of approximately 0.0008, making the anchoring gradient negligible.
- **Anchoring only takes effect after the learning rate reaches its maximum.** For s100901, the reference KL at U100, U200, and U300 is 0.009, 0.017, and 0.027 respectively, and stabilizes at 0.030–0.032 from U400 to U756, effectively constraining drift. In contrast, the non-anchored runs drift continuously away from strong policy behavior and deteriorate after U200 (see [RL Drift Diagnosis](rl_drift_analysis_20260929.md)).
