**3. Selling and execution also incur losses, but cannot be fully explained by a single selling point.**

Per game and per product, using the symmetric identity:
`revenue difference = volume difference × mean of both average prices + average-price difference × mean of both sales volumes`
After decomposition, in each game the revenue difference of −5,719 consists of:
- A quantity term of −3,882 (approximately 68%),
- A realized average price term of −1,837 (approximately 32%).
The average price term incorporates price variations due to timing of sale, quantity effects, and product output across different stages—thus, it cannot be entirely attributed to timing losses.
The average price term for strawberries is −800, and for milk is −359, both of which warrant further investigation.

The latest 10 games resulted in a total overflow of **116 units** in our warehouse, with DSM at **26 units**; there was loss, but the actual harvests of geese eggs, milk, and tomatoes have already decreased, indicating the shortfall existed before any sales were made.
Our side had **0 illegal action requests**, with actual PASS actions accounting for **4.55%**; DSM values were **400** and **4.73%**, respectively.
DSM illegal requests included picking up, watering, harvesting, and care actions, which were converted into PASS during execution. Additionally, **24** requests involving quantity adjustments were successfully executed and should not be included in the illegal action count.
These statistics do not evaluate whether a legal action is worthwhile, and "no illegal actions" cannot be taken as proof that a strategy is close to optimal.
