The market decoder computes budget features before each order, based on the current visible cash, market quotes, seed/animal fixed prices, our warehouse inventory, and the **order prefix selected by our side in this turn**. The features include remaining cash under the quote, accumulated buy-side exposure, inventory and product flow, and the small integer quantities affordable for each procurement action. The quantity readout simultaneously reads the quote expenditure, funding gap, and sell-inventory gap for each candidate quantity. These projections and readouts are learned solely through the original action likelihood, without introducing new prediction targets or supervision loss.

Budget computation uses float32. Features are recovered from existing float16 observations, and coin-level precision is not guaranteed.
Accounting estimates trade volume based on current prices: buying is limited by estimated funds, and selling is limited by estimated inventory; thus, million-scale "sell-out" requests do not generate fictitious large revenues; accumulated buy requests still retain their original request exposure.
Continuous HIRE quotes advance according to Fibonacci ratios. It is not a complete environment simulator: unit-stage inventory changes, opponent orders, and common market price impacts may cause discrepancies between estimates and settlements. **All previously valid actions remain supported; no procurement is forcibly truncated, and no opponent private state or future actions are read.**

## Formal Training and Recovery

[Unique Hydra Recipe](../configs/learning_budget.yaml) uses two H200 GPUs, 128 games per turn,
100,000 turns PPO total budget. After BC, automatically enters **128 games of pure self-play, 256 learning trajectories per turn**;
`population.fraction=0`, `pfsp=null`, `sources=[]`, `script_population=null`, `curriculum=null`.
Historical snapshots are saved for recovery and evaluation, and are not used as training opponents.
RL does not include BC/rehearsal loss. T=1, original reward, KL regularization, and global entropy coefficient remain unchanged; Actor LR `7.5e-6`,
Critic LR `1e-4`, logical batch size 5,760, dual-GPU micro-batch size 2,880.
Training seed starts from 4,210,000,000; evaluation and behavior checking seeds use a separate seed range.

PPO action sampling uses a continuously advancing CUDA random number stream, **without resetting the root seed per game, seat, or update**.
Two GPU ranks establish distinct streams only during the initialization of a new PPO phase; upon recovery, they load their respective checkpoint RNGs.
The environment seed is `train_seed + (update - 1) * games + game_index`, and it is not repeated across rounds or between two GPUs.
A fixed root seed ensures reproducibility of the entire training process, but does not mean that the same sequence of Gumbel random numbers is generated in every replay.
Evaluation uses an isolated `fork_rng`, and the evaluation seed is not retained within the training stream.

For supplementary random seed handling for users, a new GPU regression check has been added: consecutive sampling of the same observation is performed 8 times, with sequential RNG state changes. At the 3rd iteration, the RNG state is saved, the inference graph is re-captured, and subsequent 5 actions and random states are individually matched. Both the uncompiled and compiled CUDA graph paths pass the validation. In actual dual-GPU acceptance testing of U1–U4 checkpoints, the random states across two GPUs are distinct and evolve round by round, including the archived recovery from U3 to U4; the environment seed also shows no repetition. See [PPO RNG Verification](../outputs/analysis/budget-20260911/ppo-rng-checkpoints.json) and [Regression Check Logs](../outputs/analysis/budget-20260911/rng-tests.log). No issues of repeated random number reset along training paths were found, and thus no modifications to the sampling logic of already-running samples have been introduced. Random number progression alone cannot guarantee a non-concentrated policy distribution, nor can it independently explain the collapse at the opening phase.
